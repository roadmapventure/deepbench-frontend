## The pitch

`VC-SYN-001` names two things the diligence reviewer cannot do today. The first, what one answer cost, has a preserved payload waiting on `SES-369`. The second is this pass: every dollar figure DeepBench shows is DeepBench's own arithmetic, and nothing outside the platform has ever confirmed it.

Measured 2026-09-19: the IP gate (§19t) is enforcing spend walls on computed dollars — John's home row reads 1,379 calls, $12.49 against a $20 cap, 3 blocked attempts; his former IP was blocked for `spend_cap` at $9.72 against $5; four real visitors sit at $0.17–$0.39 of their $5. The AI Audit's Total Cost tile and the About screen's "every call is on the record" numbers are sums of the same book. `LOG-149` (done, v7.0.450) froze a price on rows at write time, and today the book's base rates match Anthropic's published table line for line (Fable 5.1 $10/$50, Opus 5 $5/$25, Sonnet 5 $2/$10, Haiku 4.5 $1/$5). But the ledger prices only 38 of the 329 model rows written since that ship (12%): every `call_source='session'` row on Opus 5 (105) and Fable 5.1 (67) and 97 of 117 Haiku UI rows carry no cost. And the book knows none of the modifiers that stack on top: 1-hour cache writes at 2x (priced flat at 1.25x), web search at $10 per 1,000 (unpriced; the executor already detects the blocks), US inference geography at 1.1x. No file in `api/`, `src/`, `lib/`, `scripts/` or `shared/` reads `cost_report` or an Admin key.

The proposal is **Ledger reconciled to the provider's bill.** One deterministic runner step, every cycle, calls Anthropic's `GET /v1/organizations/cost_report` for the trailing two days grouped by `description` (which parses out `model`) and writes one row per day per model into `provider_cost_daily`. One SQL view, `spend_reconciliation`, joins that to the ledger's stored `cost_usd` by day and model (`model is not null`, so librarian and deterministic rows are excluded by kind, not zeroed) and returns billed dollars, ledger dollars, the delta, and the count of model rows the ledger could not price. One line on the AI Audit beside Total Cost: "Reconciled to Anthropic: $X billed vs $Y ledger, <window>, N rows unpriced" — or "Not reconciled — no provider report" when the credential is absent (an em dash, never a matched zero). No model call anywhere in it (`VC-INVAR-005`).

Why the platform needs it, in order:

1. **It is `VC-EXIT-021` word for word** — "costs that reconcile, because diligence trust dies on one wrong number" — and it is the second half of `VC-SYN-001`'s stated gap. The acquirer's architect (`VC-EXIT-007`) does not want a cost tile; they want the tile's number tied to the invoice.
2. **It is the instrument that would have caught the 12% coverage on the day it shipped.** `LOG-149`'s claim "cost_usd is never NULL" is false for 291 of 329 model rows since v7.0.450; nothing on the platform notices, because nothing compares the ledger to anything. The view's `unpriced_rows` column is that alarm, and it fires again for every future model, modifier, or call path the book misses.
3. **It is the number John already governs on.** `VC-INVAR-020` and `VC-REJECTED-031`: any number that leaves the platform ships with its defense. The defense of a dollar is the bill. The gate blocks real people on figures nobody has checked.
4. **It is the cheapest thing on the list.** The cycle already runs on a schedule and hosts deterministic checks; the ledger already carries the four token counts; the AI Audit already renders Total Cost from one hydrated set. One table, one view, one line, one env var. Two cycles.

The honest risk is pattern 137: read as a finance chore it is an administrative expectation and the Prioritizer would send it to P10. Two things keep it P3. Where it lands — on the public audit surface the diligence engineer already reads, as a claim with its defense — and what the market does with it: Anthropic's own docs page lists six partner integrations that sell exactly this reconciliation to *developers* (CloudZero, Datadog, Grafana Cloud, Harness, Honeycomb, Vantage); I searched and found no agent-workforce product showing it to its *buyer*.
