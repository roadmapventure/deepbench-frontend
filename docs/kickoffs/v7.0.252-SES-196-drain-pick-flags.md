## The three boundaries this ship did NOT cross

- **`SES-154`'s pick-vs-retirement boundary is untouched.** The flags are in the **pick** predicate
  only. In the retirement predicate they would return `open_now = 0` and close John's standing
  directive on the runner's own say-so — the `SES-142` authorisation defect. Proven by measurement,
  not asserted: `open_now` reads **18** both pre-fix and post-fix, and the all-flagged fixture below
  returns `blocked`, never `retired`.
- **`'designed'` is not a flag.** The directive's literal words are *"`design_status` is non-null"*;
  taken literally that also skips `designed`, which step 5's table calls **explicitly not a skip**
  and step 6 calls the fast path — so the literal reading would make a drain step past its *best*
  picks. His own parenthetical enumerates the three, and the three are what shipped. **Named for
  John on the ship card as a deviation from his literal wording, not buried.**
- **`status = 'removal proposed'` was left pickable.** `SES-196`'s description lists it among the
  five blocked-prefix flags; John scoped the directive to `design_status`. It stays step 5's
  procedural skip and is carried as this ticket's **declared remainder**.

## What the prohibition was protecting, and why it survives

`SES-197`'s reason was real: filtering flagged members out of the picker stops a cycle
`record_skip()`ing them, which is what puts the ask on John's §10. **Checked live at 05:1xZ, before
the change shipped, rather than argued away** — every flagged member already carries an *unresolved*
`runner_skips` row:

| ticket | reason_kind | skip_count | last skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SES-191` | needs-desktop | 1 | 04:45Z |
| `SES-180` | needs-desktop | 8 | 04:45Z |
| `SES-181` | needs-john | 1 | 04:46Z |
| `SES-182` | needs-john | 2 | 04:49Z |

A skip row is cleared by the ticket going `done`, never by a cycle deciding it has waited long
enough, and §10 derives *still skipped* from that row plus the ticket's status. So the ask keeps its
home; what stops is the **re-skipping of a ticket already on his page** — `SES-154`'s own
one-ask-one-home boundary arriving here. **Gate C is now belt-and-braces** and stays: two homes for
one rule is this platform's standing pattern (`ck_runner_verdicts_fail_closed`, `SES-181`), and
Gate C is what fires if a future edit takes the clause back out of the picker.
