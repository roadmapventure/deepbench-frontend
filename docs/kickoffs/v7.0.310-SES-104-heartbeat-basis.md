The premise holds unchanged after eight days. Two facts the ticket did not carry, and both changed the design:

- **All 43 are closed**, and **all 43 carry a heartbeat *later* than their own `started_at`.** That is what makes the value dangerous rather than obviously wrong — it is indistinguishable from a real heartbeat, so no reader can repair it.
- **`stall_watchdog()` already coalesces.** Read from `pg_get_functiondef`, not assumed: it measures from `coalesce(heartbeat_at, started_at)` and has since `SES-194`. Probe (d) never got that treatment.

## The design decision the ticket left open, and why its own wording is wrong

The ticket offers: *"backfill `heartbeat_at` to NULL for rows that never wrote one (**or** exclude `heartbeat_at IS NULL` from (d))."* **The "or" is wrong — the two halves repair different defects and neither substitutes for the other.**

1. **The data half.** `coalesce()` is powerless against a non-NULL *wrong* value. Only NULLing the 43 rows makes the ledger say what is true: these cycles never reported.
2. **The detector half.** `heartbeat_at < now() - INTERVAL '20 minutes'` evaluates to **NULL — not true** — for a NULL heartbeat, so such a row silently leaves the tripwire and John gets no push. Meanwhile `stall_watchdog()`, which coalesces, remains free to close that same row `failed` at the 24-hour bar. **A cycle could be closed for going silent having never produced the 20-minute alert the tripwire exists to send.** Two detectors over one column disagreeing about what a missing value means is the drift this repo keeps paying for.

Probe (d) therefore adopts `stall_watchdog()`'s own expression, so the 20-minute detector and the 24-hour closer cannot drift apart.

## What shipped

- **`docs/runbooks/runner-cycle.md`** — probe (d) filters and measures on `coalesce(heartbeat_at, started_at)` and projects `never_reported`, with the rationale and two boundaries stated inline.
- **Migration `ses104_null_never_reported_heartbeats`** — before-images for all 43 rows (§19v), then NULL. The predicate is **two-part on purpose**: the heartbeat statement writes `heartbeat_at` **and** `last_step` in one `UPDATE`, so `last_step IS NULL` proves no cycle ever ran it. It raises rather than guessing if the count is not 43.
- **`tests/regression/SES-104-heartbeat-basis.js`** — 4 runbook clauses each with its own negative control, the `SES-158` vacuity meta-check, a semantic check on both bases, and a credential-gated data invariant.

## The edit this ship forbids

**Moving `never_reported` out of the projection and into the `WHERE`.** A never-reporting open peer is *more* worth pushing, not less — it has not reached its first step boundary — and the flag changes only the push's wording ("has not reported since it started" rather than "frozen since its last step"). Filtering on it rebuilds the exact invisibility this ticket fixed.

## Not done, named rather than left to be found

**The column's `DEFAULT now()` is deliberately kept.** It is what makes a fresh row's basis equal its own `started_at` — the correct basis — and dropping it changes every future `INSERT` for no observable gain under the coalesce. The honest consequence: **on today's schema a live row cannot carry NULL**, so the NULL arm guards the 43 historical rows and any future explicit NULL. This ship removes a divergence; it does not patch a hot path. Claiming otherwise would overstate what the measurement supports.

The 2 rows carrying `last_step IS NULL` beside their own `DEFAULT`-now() start-time heartbeat are correctly **spared** — their basis is already honest.
