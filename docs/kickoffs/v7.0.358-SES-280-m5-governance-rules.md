## 2. CONTEXT

A 2026-09-01 accounting review measured the drain and found the numbers that motivate every rule below. This week (since the 08-28 07:00Z weekly reset): **191,703,000 tokens across 140 cycles — 138,703,000 (72.4%) shipped 55 tickets, 53,000,000 (27.6%) shipped nothing.** The 69 non-shipping cycles touched only **12 distinct tickets** (~5.75 cycles per stuck ticket). Average completed Selfbuild ticket costs **1,663,212 tokens** across 80 done/delivered rows.

Three structural defects surfaced alongside those numbers:

1. **No scope fence.** Drain eligibility is a directive-named list (`runner_drain_scope`), not a structural property. Nothing prevents non-Selfbuild work entering the unattended lane.
2. **The rolling-wave rule is prose only.** `Selfbuild M5`'s own design gate `SES-184` sits at `design_status = 'needs-john'` while M5 already holds 10 open member tickets — two of them (`SES-276`, `SES-277`) filed 2026-09-01 by a runner cycle. The charter says members are filed at the gate, "never speculatively"; nothing enforces it.
3. **Ticket↔cycle attribution leaks.** `runner_cycles.item_id` is free text with no FK: this week **4 of 71 shipped cycles carry no `item_id`**, and 6 more carry an `item_id` matching no `backlog_items` row. Per-ticket cost queries silently drop them. Separately, 23 `done` Selfbuild tickets carry a null `session_ref`.

**B3 is the direct conflict.** It is live and script-enforced: *"Order the backlog queue by tier, then priority class P1→P10, then beta-first, then newest-to-oldest within class."* John's filing-lane rule requires tickets filed **before** 2026-08-21 to take the priority lane — the opposite ordering. Both cannot be live. Per the charter's transition rule ("a rule changes only via a shipped ticket whose own commit lands the replacement, retires the old rule … and leaves no commit where neither is in force"), B3's supersession ships in the same push as `M5-02`.

**Phase split.** This ticket is **Phase 1: encode**. It lands the rule rows, the canonical doc, and B3's retirement. **Phase 2 (follow-up ticket) wires script enforcement into the pick path** (`drain_epic_next()`, `recompute_backlog_queue()`). The split is forced by the 3-file / 4-task session cap and is also correct sequencing — John asked to encode first and review what is and isn't working before enforcement hardens. Rules land with their intended `enforcement` value recorded; the `script` ones are not executable until Phase 2, and this kickoff does not claim otherwise.

**Held back deliberately:** the enhancement-lane rules (admission test, weekly enhancement cap, promotion path) are **not** in this ticket. They await John's decision on the cap percentage, raised 2026-09-01 and unanswered. Filing them half-decided would cost a second amendment cycle under the transition rule.
