## The problem, stated as the directive found it

`B31` (`v7.0.106`) built the `runner_lease` singleton because a *read* (`SELECT id FROM
runner_cycles WHERE ended_at IS NULL`) cannot serialise against a concurrent write: cycle
`e36d4379` selected zero open cycles ~17 seconds after `4da5a7bd` had already inserted its row,
both picked the same top-of-queue item, and both built `ADM-1 v1`. The single-row `UPDATE` fixed
that race at **claim time**.

It fixed nothing after claim time. **The lease was asserted once, at step 0, and never again.**
Nothing between the claim and the push made a cycle re-check that it still owned the run.

That hole is only reachable if a stolen-from cycle keeps running — and step 0's own rationale
said it could not:

> *"Longest real cycle to date is ~18 minutes against a 3-hour cadence, so a stolen lease means
> the holder is dead, not slow."*

**That sentence is false, and the measurement is unambiguous.** Cycle `633fe486` opened
`05:07:15Z`; its 45-minute lease expired ~`05:52Z` and was stolen; it was **still executing
normally at `13:10:51Z`** — `select now()` read from inside that session — ~8 hours later, having
completed its probe, subagent delegation, edits, kickoff doc, build, regression 31/31 and snapshot
export. A cloud session can be suspended and resumed across wall-clock gaps that are **invisible
from inside it**. The session experienced minutes; eight hours passed.

So the failure is live: **a stolen-from cycle does not stop, because it does not know.**
`633fe486` came **one command short** of pushing a duplicate of already-shipped work to `dev` —
the exact double-build the lease exists to prevent, reached by a route the lease does not cover.
It stopped only because it happened to `git fetch origin dev` at the ship point and see the
successor's commits. That is luck, not a control. The standing prohibition *"never build without
holding the lease"* had **no enforcement point after step 0**.

## What shipped

One file, four edits, all in `docs/runbooks/runner-cycle.md`:

1. **Step 0, TTL bullet — corrected.** The "dead, not slow" sentence is retired and quoted as
   retired (never silently rewritten), with `633fe486`'s timings as the disproof. A steal now
   means the holder is **silent**, not dead — the same correction step 0b already makes for
   `failed`, applied to the lease that produced it. The TTL itself is unchanged: it has to exist,
   or a quiet cycle wedges the routine until a human notices.
2. **Step 0, new block — the re-assertion gate**, defined once:
   ```sql
   SELECT holder, held_since FROM public.runner_lease
    WHERE id = 1 AND holder = '<your cycle id>';
   ```
   1 row → proceed. 0 rows → stolen from, and the procedure is spelled out rather than left to
   inference: **do not push; do not claim a counter; do NOT release the lease** (holder-guarded, so
   it matches nothing anyway — but attempting it is how a cycle talks itself into touching a
   successor's row, which step 0b forbids); **close and annotate your OWN row**; **push your session
   branch** so the work survives the container and is cherry-pickable (`ba8f2ce3`/`633fe486` both
   did this and their work was recovered, not redone); and **check whether the successor already
   shipped your item before discarding**, so the discard is deliberate.
3. **Step 6 — wired at the counter claim.** A version claimed after you lost the lease is a
   permanent gap at best, a collision at worst.
4. **Step 7 — wired as a hard gate on the push**, keeping the existing `git fetch origin dev`
   explicitly. They catch **different** failures: the fetch catches a successor that shipped
   without ever taking your lease; the re-assertion catches the case where the lease moved.
   Also added to the standing prohibitions.

## Scope discipline

The directive's Finding 2 (`.claude/` writes are not reliably blocked; test the suspension
confound) is **deliberately not acted on**. It was filed at 13:12Z; `v7.0.122` shipped at 14:02Z
on **John's own testimony** — *"Those sessions came back alive because I opened them and allowed
permissions"* — which supersedes it, and step 0 explicitly says not to re-soften that rule on
another latency reading. Note the two are not even in conflict on the mechanism: suspension and a
human-cleared permission prompt both predict exactly what `633fe486` observed. What settles it is
that John saw the prompt, and no cycle can.

The directive's optional extension — *"consider also having a stolen-from cycle check whether its
own work was already shipped by the successor before discarding"* — **is** included, as a bullet
in the stolen-from procedure. It costs one `git fetch` and is what turns an accidental discard
into a deliberate one.

## What this does NOT claim

The gate closes the window between claim and push. It does **not** make a stolen-from cycle stop
*mid-build* — it will still burn its remaining steps before reaching a gate — and it does not
detect a successor that never took the lease (the `git fetch` does that, which is why both stay).
Making the TTL itself suspension-aware is a larger change and is not attempted here.
