## 4. The five questions (answer all, in this order)

**Q1 — Where does the project sit against the charter?** Take the charter's milestone list and its "Definition of success — project-end acceptance" section. For every criterion, verify its actual state with primary evidence (a table row, a file, a CI run, a script) — never a checkmark or a prose claim. Output: per-criterion verdict — MET (evidence), NOT MET (what's missing), or CLAIMED-BUT-UNVERIFIABLE (what evidence would settle it).

**Q2 — What is genuinely left to close the project?** From Q1's NOT-MET list plus the charter's own open questions and `runner_skips`/open `SES-*` tickets in `backlog_items` tied to Selfbuild epics: the complete remaining-work list. Note items already waiting on John (e.g. the two `SES-256` rollback-drill decisions recorded in `CLAUDE-STATE.md`) as decisions-pending, not new work.

**Q3 — Drift.** Where does prose disagree with the built system? Start by auditing the truth tripwire (`SES-176`) and reviewer lane (`SES-181`): what do they actually check, and what governance surface do they NOT cover? Then hunt in the uncovered surface: charter vs `governance_rules` rows (status flips, retired rules still stated as live, live rules missing from the registry), runbooks vs actual scripts/SQL, GOVERNANCE-MODES.md vs runner behavior recorded in `runner_cycles`. Every drift finding needs both sides quoted/cited: the prose claim and the contradicting system fact.

**Q4 — 80/20 discipline.** Classify every open item from Q2 and every proposed fix from Q3 into exactly one bucket: **(a) CLOSE** — needed for the happy path to be trustworthy and the charter honestly closable; or **(b) LATER** — edge-case hardening, polish, or coverage of rare paths, safe to park. Bias hard toward (b): the bar for (a) is "the project cannot honestly be called closed and working without it," not "this would make it better." End with a recommendation: the shortest credible ordered path to project close, and the explicit parked list.

**Q5 — The CEO summary.** A standalone section, ~400–600 words, zero DeepBench jargon (no ticket IDs, no table names, no §-references). Written for a CEO deciding whether this spend was right and whether to keep investing. Cover, in business terms: what the company got (an autonomous build capability with governance — describe what it does in outcomes, e.g. work ships on a schedule without a human driving, every change is recorded and reversible, bad changes roll back automatically, the owner governs by exception through a briefing rather than by supervision); what risks the governance removed and what each protection is worth; what it means for scaling (cost of adding work vs headcount); and an honest one-paragraph statement of what is not yet done and the cost of finishing. Confidence through evidence, not cheerleading — if something isn't true yet, say so.
