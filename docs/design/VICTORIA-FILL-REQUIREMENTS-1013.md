# Victoria fill requirements (working list, 2026-10-13)

Status: DESIGN, being walked through one rule at a time with John. Nothing here is built. These are "Victoria rules": where one conflicts with an older rule, the Victoria rule wins, and code guards must be changed to match (a prompt cannot override a database guard).

## Decided
1. **Goal.** Victoria keeps the builders working. Every night each active project holds at least ten tickets scored 3 or higher that a routine can take: ready to build, or needing a design before build. Not countable: blocked, parked (scored 1 with "PARKED (0)"), or waiting on John (design or gate card). Her work is tickets only; findings are not tickets and do not count.
2. **Trigger.** Checked once a day. Any active project under ten countable tickets prompts her; she is not prompted when none is.
3. **Floor, not cap.** Ten is a floor. She fills to ten, 5s first, then 4s, then 3s. A 5 that fits a project moves in even at ten (the project then holds eleven or more). No swapping a 3 out.
4. **Only her grades count.** A score with no Victoria `requirement-check` record counts as ungraded and is regraded before it can move. (Checked 2026-10-13: all 136 open tickets scored 3+ in holding lists and active projects have her record.)
5. **Fit before grade.** She reads a ticket once against all active projects' charters. Fits one: grade it, and move it if 3+. Fits none: do not grade; stamp it with the date and "fits no active project"; re-check only when a project becomes active or its charter changes.
6. **Creation date = `filed_at`.** `created_at` is the database insert time (two bulk loads on 2026-08-20 cover 308 tickets filed 2026-06-07 to 2026-08-12).
7. **Order within a score.** Filed 14 days ago or less: oldest first. Filed more than 14 days ago: newest first, after the first group.
8. **Freshness.** Any ticket filed more than 14 days ago is revalidated before it moves in. (2026-10-13: of 120 scored 3+ in the Development Manager's and Auditor's lists, 68 are 14 days or newer and 52 are older; 29 of those 52 were already revalidated in the last 14 days.)

9. **Out of 3s: no notification, no code.** John does not rely on notifications (none has reached him). Each day, when he asks for the previous night's run report he also asks for the current queue, and spots a low project himself. The morning report shows, per active project: the countable ticket count, and any project Victoria's latest run line names as run dry. Whether a project should stay active is John's call from that report.

10. **Off-limits to her moves (agreed 2 of 3).** She never moves a ticket John named into a different project, and never moves a ticket into a paused project (e.g. agent-training). Claimed tickets: a live claim (`claimed_by`) stops her; a claim older than 6 hours counts as released (John, 2026-10-13) and the ticket is then a normal candidate, its design or partial work kept. (Now: 3 Development Manager tickets carry claims over 6 hours old; 13 tickets in the two holding lists are `partial`.)
12. **Move from any source (rule 6) is a requirement, not a question.** Tickets from the Auditor, Development Manager, Nathan, Jerry or John are all eligible on the same terms. How the lock (`epic_lock_guard`), `accepts_findings` and the validator are changed to allow it is a Designer call.
11. **Child tickets take the parent's score.** A ticket that blocks a parent in an active project (`blocked_by`) moves into the same project, counts toward ten, and is picked at the higher of its own score and the parent's. It cannot be graded low and left behind. (2026-10-13: AGT-159 (5), AGT-313 (4), AGT-291 (3) and AGT-158 (3) in dev-manager-capabilities are blocked by AGT-133, AGT-312 and AGT-151, all `delivered` and never `done`, a finaliser-wedge symptom; dev-manager-capabilities may hold zero countable tickets. Not confirmed that the pick treats a `delivered` blocker as still blocking.)
