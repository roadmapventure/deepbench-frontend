# Market Map — Competitors, Neighbors, and DeepBench's Whitespace

Defines **P2 - Inventive** concretely and feeds the Invent engine (§19v): the nearest comparable products, what they do and don't do, and where genuine whitespace is.

## Category 1 — Developer orchestration frameworks (code-first)

- [C-MAP-1] (MED) The consolidated 2026 framework field is LangGraph (largest enterprise production footprint), CrewAI (fastest prototyping, role-based crews), Microsoft AutoGen (research/conversation-pattern leader), plus OpenAI's Swarm/AgentKit for narrow handoffs. — *grounds:* outside: presenc.ai "Multi-Agent Orchestration Frameworks 2026"; turing.com framework comparison; dev.to LangGraph-vs-CrewAI-vs-AutoGen 2026 guide.
- [C-MAP-2] (MED) ~28% of production multi-agent deployments in 2026 still use custom orchestration — the frameworks have not won the harness layer. — *grounds:* outside: presenc.ai 2026 research note.
- [C-MAP-3] (HIGH) What they don't do: agents are code artifacts — changing an agent is a software release. DeepBench's agents are data rows (Skills → Capabilities → Agents, many-to-many), so improving quality is "a training operation, not a software release." — *grounds:* ARCHITECTURE.md §0 [LOCKED], §2; the Generic Capability Executor (§19b: "never hand-roll a capability route; everything is data").
- [C-MAP-4] (MED) These frameworks are DeepBench's infrastructure peers, not competitors for a buyer: none ships an end-user product, personas, or deliverables — but any diligence reviewer will benchmark DeepBench's harness against them. — *grounds:* inference; C-EXIT-7 skeptic-eye bar.

## Category 2 — Enterprise agent platforms (suite-attached)

- [C-MAP-5] (MED) Salesforce Agentforce, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Moveworks (ServiceNow), and UiPath anchor the enterprise segment: agents attached to an existing suite's data and distribution. — *grounds:* outside: noca.ai "Digital Employees: Top 10 Platforms for 2026"; erp.today "AI Agents as Digital Coworkers" (2026).
- [C-MAP-6] (MED) What they don't do: cross-suite generality and transparent internals — their agents are configuration inside a closed suite; audit/attribution exists for admins, not as an end-user product surface. — *grounds:* inference from outside sources above; contrast with DeepBench's AI Audit screen (§19m) being a first-class user screen.
- [C-MAP-7] (MED) The market frame is large and validating: AI agents market ~$12B in early 2026, projected ~$52.6B by 2030; 40% of applications expected to deploy autonomous agents by end of 2026. — *grounds:* outside: litslink.com "AI Agents Market Statistics 2026"; noimosai.com digital-workforce outlook.

## Category 3 — Agent-workforce / "digital employee" products (nearest in spirit)

- [C-MAP-8] (MED) The closest-in-concept products sell named digital workers for one function: Sierra (customer service), 11x and Artisan (AI SDRs, $75M+ raised behind 11x), Lindy and Relevance AI (generalist assistant/workforce builders). — *grounds:* outside: quiq.com "Sierra AI Competitors 2026"; 11x.ai comparison guides; noca.ai top-10 digital-employee platforms.
- [C-MAP-9] (MED) What they don't do: (a) they are single-function verticals, not a bench of collaborating specialists producing a joint deliverable; (b) the persona is marketing skin over one pipeline, not a data-modeled competency; (c) inter-agent orchestration is invisible or nonexistent. — *grounds:* outside sources above; inference; contrast with §19d Agent Loop + Michelle-brokered delegation (RULE #1: no agent's data names another agent).
- [C-MAP-10] (MED) Their weakness is DeepBench's stated lesson: output volume without inspectable quality ("zero quality leads despite high outreach volume" reported against AI SDR products) — exactly the final-output-only failure John rejected with the house/contractors analogy. — *grounds:* outside: 11x.ai/getbreakout.ai comparison content re Artisan reviews; John 2026-07-16 contractor analogy → §19r/§19s.

## What P2 - Inventive means concretely (rewritten 2026-09-14 — supersedes C-MAP-24 through C-MAP-27)

**Superseded — kept for history, not current:** the four bullets that used to live here (C-MAP-24 through C-MAP-27) built the P2 test on the whitespace-lanes section above, which John rejected the same day (see the section's own correction note). The six tests below replace them, confirmed by John one at a time in chat, 2026-09-14.

A feature is **P2 - Inventive** only if it passes all three of tests 1–3. Tests 4–5 further rank how strong a P2 candidate is; test 6 is a soft flag, not a disqualifier.

- [C-MAP-28] (HIGH) **Test 1 — Not-yet-built.** The feature does not exist in DeepBench today. Deepening something already built or already underway is P5 - Enhancements (`ARCHITECTURE.md:2544`), never P2, no matter how new the deepening is. — *grounds:* John, live in chat, 2026-09-14: "these are enhancements to existing features, and should go to that class."
- [C-MAP-29] (HIGH) **Test 2 — Real demand.** Someone has actually asked for it or hit its absence — John's own stated want, a trusted-beta-tester ask, a live demo-evaluator's question, or documented outside pain (community forums, industry reports) — not speculation about what might be nice. — *grounds:* John, live in chat, 2026-09-14: "what people are asking for."
- [C-MAP-30] (HIGH) **Test 3 — The real competitors structurally won't build it.** The competitor set is Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude — not the vertical multi-agent frameworks in the categories above (John, live in chat, 2026-09-14: "I see co-pilot, chat-gpt, and claude as my biggest competitors"). A candidate passes only if it is too vertical, too accountability/trust-heavy for a mass-market consumer product, or in conflict with that competitor's own business model (e.g., a paid resale marketplace cuts into their own API revenue) — not merely "they haven't shipped it yet." **Named trap:** multi-agent orchestration, persistent agent memory, and agent self-training/self-improvement automatically fail this test — frontier labs are actively racing to ship these as infrastructure, and DeepBench cannot out-resource that race. — *grounds:* John, live in chat, 2026-09-14: "as soon as they allow multi-agent communication, agent knowledge storage and behavior/reasoning self improvements/training... deepbench is no longer valid."
- [C-MAP-31] (HIGH) **Test 4 — Durability.** Even where test 3 holds today, ask how long a well-resourced fast-follower would need to clone it if they changed their mind. A feature clonable from a screenshot in a sprint is weak P2; one that requires years of accumulated data, a domain relationship, or case history is strong P2. — *grounds:* Claude's recommendation (SaaS/moat-strategy synthesis — counter-positioning is real but not permanent), confirmed by John in chat 2026-09-14.
- [C-MAP-32] (HIGH) **Test 5 — Compounding asset.** Does shipping it make DeepBench's own accumulating asset (trained Skills/Capabilities content, provenance/attribution history, case history) more valuable the longer the platform runs — or is it a one-time UI/workflow feature a competitor could clone without inheriting any of that asset? Ties directly to §0b: "the deeper the DEEP, the more valuable the BENCH." — *grounds:* Claude's recommendation, confirmed by John in chat 2026-09-14.
- [C-MAP-33] (MED, soft flag not a disqualifier) **Test 6 — Beachhead fit.** Does it deepen the specific vertical DeepBench is actually positioned to win — government procurement/agencies via the NIGP lineage (`customer.md` C-CUST-16) — rather than being generically novel with no named buyer? A feature can still be worth building without this, but it ranks lower than one that hits the beachhead. — *grounds:* Claude's recommendation, confirmed by John in chat 2026-09-14.
- [C-MAP-34] (HIGH) **Whitespace decays — recheck, don't lock forever.** A "the real competitors structurally won't build this" claim (test 3) can go stale overnight — a lab ships something, a startup launches. Every P2 classification carries an implicit recheck, not a permanent status. — *grounds:* carried forward from the retired C-MAP-27; still true, now attached to the right test.
- [C-MAP-35] (HIGH) **Test 7 — Intelligence-native, not code-native.** The strongest P2 candidates are not UI screens, data tables, or deterministic workflow logic (`enable_web_search`/schema/routing — the kind of thing the Capability Badge Rule, `ARCHITECTURE.md §12`, already separates from real AI work). The bar: does the value come from the model's own reasoning, decisioning, and communication — replacing a judgment call, a direction-setting decision, or an answer a human previously had to personally provide — rather than from code someone wrote? A feature can pass tests 1–6 and still be a "coded" feature dressed as inventive; the strongest candidates need no new deterministic logic at all, only a new *framing* for capability the model already has. — *grounds:* John, live in chat, 2026-09-14: "something that is more intelligence oriented - shows ai reasoning, decisioning, and communication - something that does not have to be coded, and would previously take a human to direct, decide, and provide an answer."

## Real-world validation (live WebSearch, 2026-09-14 — not fabricated, see Sources)

Checked test 3's premise against outside evidence before trusting it: agent accountability/audit-trail is a real, unsolved, currently-monetizing industry gap, not just internal inference.

- A W3C community group proposed standardizing "agentic integrity verification" only in March 2026 — this is unsolved industry-wide, not something Copilot/ChatGPT/Claude have shipped. ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/community/blog/2026/03/30/proposed-group-agentic-integrity-verification-specification-community-group))
- "There is currently no agreed-upon way to prove what the agent actually did, in what sequence, and whether the record has been tampered with" — and startups (e.g. Conduit) are already selling tamper-proof AI-agent audit trails as a standalone product. ([dev.to](https://dev.to/veritaschain/the-grok-scandal-proves-ai-needs-cryptographic-audit-trails-not-just-content-moderation-3ia6))
- McKinsey 2026: ~two-thirds of organizations cite security/risk concerns as the top barrier to scaling agentic AI; 80% have already seen risky agent behavior — trust is the adoption blocker, a paying-customer-shaped problem. ([digitalstrategy-ai.com](https://digitalstrategy-ai.com/mckinsey-ai-trust-agentic-era))
- Counter-signal, ruled out: "goldfish memory" (agents forgetting between sessions) is the most-complained-about issue, but it's exactly the lane frontier labs are racing to fix (test 3's named trap) — real pain, wrong lane for DeepBench. ([The Goldfish with a User Interface](https://theagentarchitect.substack.com/p/goldfish-memory-ai-agents))
