## 2. The decisive finding — a close-out-only ruling is half a fix

`docs/runbooks/runner-cycle.md` L219-226, read this session:

> `nothing_pickable` **is the unbounded one** — an empty drain means no fire boots and therefore no
> fire harvests until an attended session runs.

A refusing fire never starts a cycle. So a ruling step placed inside the cycle (step 7b, close-out)
can only rule the cards *that cycle wrote* — and the moment the last card leaves the board with no
pickable work behind it, no later fire exists to rule it. The ticket asks for both halves ("at the
end of the cycle that wrote it **and again at boot for any older one**"), and the boot half cannot
be a step inside a cycle that never boots. That is why the design changes `runner_should_boot()`.

Three shapes were considered:

1. **Drop the SES-424 exclusion when a card is old.** Rejected: it silently converts a gate into a
   timer, which is exactly the B23 "silence parks a card forever" failure inverted into "silence
   ships an ungated build." A gate must be *ruled*, not aged out.
2. **Rule only at close-out.** Rejected: half the ticket, and the half that does not fix the
   measured outage.
3. **A new boot reason, `gate_cards_to_rule`, after every wall.** Taken. The gate already answers
   "is there anything to do at all"; ruling a card is something to do. Placing the branch after
   `weekly_wall` / `weekly_pace` / `no_budget_row` keeps the never-act-past-a-wall property intact,
   and `detail.mode='rule-cards-only'` lets step 0 take the short path rather than paying the
   473 KB briefing read a refusal exists to avoid.

**The loop this design had to close.** A card the manager pushes to John must stay
`decision IS NULL` — that is what keeps its ticket out of the pick path, which is correct, because
John's call really is blocking. But an undecided card is also what makes the gate say
`gate_cards_to_rule`. Left alone, a pushed card re-fires a ruling cycle forever, one judgment-lane
call each time. The exit: the gate counts only cards with **no open `runner_questions` row** whose
`qid` is `'gate-card-' || left(card_id::text, 8)`. `qid` carries a unique constraint (measured), the
status vocabulary is `open`/`answered` (measured, 40 rows), and John answering flips the card back
into the rulable set with no sweep and no timer — self-maintaining, `pattern:16`. `flag_slug` was
the other candidate and was rejected: its three live values are feature-flag slugs
(`chi-84-step-chip-tap`, `agt-69-governance-section`, `log-128-automated-caller-split`), so reusing
it would collide a platform word with an existing meaning (`pattern:118`).

## 3. Blast radius — stated plainly, because this is a machine closing a person's decisions

**What it may write.** `runner_items.decision` / `decision_reason` / `decided_at` on a card that is
currently NULL, and on the card's ticket: `status='removal proposed'`, `design_status` (cleared, or
`'needs-desktop'`), `scope_rationale` (appended). Nothing else, on 2 tables.

**What it may never write.** `agents.is_active` (John's hire card, §19v P5 and Guard A). Any budget
or spend row. Any push, any branch, `main`. A card already decided. A card whose subject is one of
John's four reserved calls — money, production (dev→main), hiring or switching agents on, ratifying
a bar he set — which becomes a `runner_questions` row and **no card write at all**. Those four are
`docs/WORKING-WITH-JOHN.md` L82 (the Decision Authority Matrix's last row, decision `d496fb07`) and
`scripts/audit-review.js`'s `JOHN_CALLS` constant, which already names five: `rules`, `money`,
`production`, `hiring`, `switch`. The kickoff's `john` ruling reuses that constant rather than
restating it — one home, `pattern:14`.

**Where the authority comes from, and why this is not a grant.** `MANAGER-AUTHORITY-MATRIX` row 3,
live in `public.governance_rules` and rendered into `docs/WORKING-WITH-JOHN.md`, already says: *"What
a build finds … and gate cards (runner_items.kind = gated_before_build): the manager's, within
John's standing rules and the class caps; a card whose subject is one of John's calls or rulings
becomes a runner_questions row and is counted."* Ruled by John 2026-09-16, decision `d496fb07`. The
authority has been live for eight days with no capability behind it; AGT-127 supplies the hands, not
the permission. `pattern:166` — no rule of John's is weakened here.

**How a wrong ruling is undone — and the asymmetry nobody should discover later.** Read from
`pg_get_functiondef('reverse_decision')` this session, Guard B:

> `if v_ri_kind = 'gated_before_build' or (r.prior->>'kind') = 'gated_before_build' then … REFUSED
> -- a kind='gated_before_build' card … survives every reversal.`

So `reverse_decision(<handle>, 'John', '<why>')` inside the 72-hour M6-02 window **does** restore the
ticket-side band (`backlog_items` is on the allowlist) and marks the decision reversed — but it
**does not** clear the card's `decision` stamp, by deliberate design (`SES-364`: the card is step
8d's idempotence key and the evidence a gate review happened). The consequence, stated rather than
left to be found: a reversed ruling gives the ticket back its old status but leaves it *pickable*,
because the card still reads as decided. Re-gating is therefore a **new card**, not an erasure. The
driver prints both lines — the `reverse_decision(...)` call and the `insert into runner_items …`
re-gate — so whoever reverses has the whole handle in front of them. Also worth knowing: `runner_items`
has no `updated_at`, so a working Reverse reads `restored 0, restored_unverified 1` — re-read the row
rather than trusting the count (`SES-364` note on the seven new tables).

## 4. Why this is one cycle and not a slice

AGT-88 was sliced into AGT-88/AGT-109 because its second half was a separate mechanism with its own
QA. Here the two halves — rule at close-out, rule at boot — share one SQL function, one driver, one
capability and one test file; splitting them ships a capability nothing invokes on the path that
matters, which is the `pattern:66` minimum-useful-slice test failing in the other direction. The
honest shape is 7 repo files and 8 tasks against caps of 20 and 21, so there is no cap pressure
forcing a slice either. **This kickoff is the whole ticket.**

Two things deliberately left out, each with a reason rather than an omission:

- **No change to `prime_directive_queue()`.** The exclusion is right; the gap was that nothing
  cleared it. `pattern:8` — narrowest layer that works.
- **No new agent and no `is_active` flip.** `devmanager` already exists with `dm-identity`,
  `dm-behavior`, `dm-guardrails` and three `dm-knowledge-*` rows, all reused by the new capability's
  `capability_skill_profiles` links. So `AGENT-ROW-AGREED-TICKET`'s second limb covers the writes
  (this ticket names them) and no hire card is owed. Measured: `review-audit-worklist` links 6 Skill
  rows, `run-project` links 7; the new capability links the same 6 plus its own Intent row.

## 5. The precedents this build copies rather than invents

- **`scripts/audit-review.js` + `public.apply_audit_review()`** (AGT-86 slice 2b/9b) — the SQL
  function owns the write, its before-images and one reversible decision; the script validates and
  never writes a table. `validateReview()` mirrors the function's refusals with the same texts so a
  `--dry-run` shows the whole list offline. `apply_gate_rulings` / `validateRulings()` are the same
  two halves.
- **`scripts/run-project.js`** (AGT-68, SES-403) — the two-invocation shape: pass one writes state
  and prints the assembled prompt, **exit 3 = awaiting the answer**; pass two re-reads everything
  live and refuses on drift. Its five stated refusals carry over, especially (1) *never assemble a
  prompt yourself* — `assemblePrompt()` and `renderAssembly()` are imported — and (2) *never omit
  `intent_slug`*, because `db-assembly.js` filters every Intent Skill out when it is null (AA-188),
  producing a prompt with no output contract that still looks complete.
- **`shared/ai-patterns.js`** — `SES-338` and `AGT-86 8a` both shipped a capability with Skill rows
  and no catalog entry, and both were found by `scripts/agent-log.js` *refusing every log row*
  ("not a SERVICE_CATALOG slug"). `.claude/rules/capability-logging.md` makes the Layer-3 log
  mandatory, so the entry is part of this build, not a follow-up.
