The ticket's line "`verifier.js --regrade` can only turn an approve into a block, never the
reverse" is not quite the mechanism. `--regrade` (SES-403) is built to turn a block into an
approve. It cannot fire here because `public.regradable_ships()` requires every CI job the red
gates map to to be `success` in the newest `ref='dev'` conclusion, and `regression` maps to
`Tripwire + regression (blocking)`, which is `failure` at dev head and has been standing red.
So the lane is starved, not inverted: it grades CI's **absolute** job conclusion, which is the
same defect AGT-170 fixed in the local gate and nowhere else. Worth correcting on the ticket.

## Why CI cannot be the cheap source

`ci.yml`'s `report-conclusion` job composes its payload with `jq` from two job results only.
`ci_run_conclusions` is (`commit_sha`, `run_id`, `jobs`, `concluded_at`, `ref`) — 607 rows, all
`ref='dev'`, newest `36367343688` at 01:52Z — and `jobs` is a two-element array of name +
conclusion. No test names anywhere. Teaching CI to publish its `[FAIL]` names would make FUTURE
shas delta-gradable for free, but it does nothing for the 71 already shipped. Filed as a
candidate, not designed here.

## The finding that reshaped the plan: `graded_sha` is not the ticket's tree

Since SES-336 the Builder pushes before the verifier runs, so `graded_sha` is dev HEAD at grade
time. Example: AGT-199 (v7.0.656) records `351e2aff`, whose subject is `v7.0.653 AGT-186 …` — a
peer's commit that landed after AGT-199's own two (`6c52b474`, `058312ad`). Any plan that checks
out `graded_sha` as "the tree after the ship" measures a peer's diff too.

## The resolution rule, and the three variants measured

Rule chosen: own commits = ancestors of HEAD whose subject BEGINS with the verdict's `version`
plus a space AND contains the `backlog_id` plus a space. `ship` = newest own, `base` = parent of
oldest own, clean iff `git rev-list base..ship` holds only own commits.

| variant | clean | refused |
|---|---|---|
| version + id (chosen) | **56** | 15 |
| id only | 45 | 26 |

id-only loses because later commits mention earlier ids in their subjects. The 15 refused are 14
interleaved (a peer's commit inside the range) plus AGT-202, whose latest verdict carries
`version` NULL (its previous verdict has `v7.0.636`; a fallback was rejected — one rule, fail
closed, and a named refusal is cheaper than a silently-wrong pair).

Also measured: 68 of the 70 distinct `graded_sha`s are ancestors of HEAD; `SES-388` `93b99061`
and `AGT-172` `bd19639d` are absent from history entirely. Both still resolve by subject, which
is a second argument for the subject rule over the sha.

## Cost, measured rather than estimated

The 56 clean pairs name **89 distinct trees**, not 112 and not the ticket's 142: consecutive
ships share a boundary tree. At the ticket's own ~20 min/run that is ~30 h, not ~47 h. Naming it
here because slice 3's pacing is The Development Manager's decision and it should start from a
measured number. Caveat for slice 2: sharing a tree across pairs puts the two legs of some pair
hours apart, and the suite reads live Supabase state, so world drift can inject a phantom
newly-red. A per-ticket back-to-back pair (112 runs) avoids that; the trade is slice 2's call.

## Why slice 1 buys nothing on the board, deliberately

The pre-boot gate predicted 4 cycles against 11% weekly headroom. Slice 1 runs no suite, writes
no verdict and closes no ticket — it converts an unmeasured 47-hour plan into 56 named pairs, 15
named refusals and an 89-tree cost. That is the cheapest thing that makes the rest schedulable,
and the resolver is the one genuinely novel mechanism in the ticket, so it is proven alone before
anything expensive is retrofitted onto it.

## The close path, confirmed rather than assumed

`backlog_done_requires_verdict()` reads the LATEST verdict by `created_at`; a block closes only
over a non-reversed `ship`/`ticket-status` decision or John's Accept dated at or after it.
`record_ship_decision()` refuses any verdict that is not `approve`. `runner_verdicts` has no
unique key on `backlog_id`, and `ck_runner_verdicts_fail_closed` makes `approve` exactly equal to
all three gates green. So a NEW approve row + `record_ship_decision()` + `status='done'` is the
whole close path for slice 2/3, with the original block row untouched.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Re-grade from the cycle notes / ship reports** (the hand-quoted "same 17 by name" lines).
  That is exactly the artifact AGT-170 exists because it could not be trusted — two numbers from
  two different graders. Re-blessing them would launder the defect.
- **A `regrade_cohort` table.** The list is written once and never updated; its progress is
  derivable live from `runner_verdicts`. A table adds a migration and a grant surface
  (`.claude/rules/supabase-column-grants.md`) for no readback the file cannot give.
- **A runbook step in slice 1.** `docs/runbooks/runner-cycle.md` is 380,862 of 381,000 bytes —
  138 free. Any wiring there must buy bytes first; that belongs with slice 3's pacing.
