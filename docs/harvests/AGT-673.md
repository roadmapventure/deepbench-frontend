## 4. Why the design is not a way around the check

CLAUDE.md SES-019 and redesign-after-refusal.md clause 4 forbid retrying a refused action, routing it through another tool, or wording a step to avoid the check. Tested against that:

- This build sends no migration, re-sends nothing that was refused, and changes no launch text, prompt or setting the check reads.
- The rule it writes down is the mechanism the runbook already names (`docs/runbooks/runner-cycle.md:2839`: capture, then `apply_migration`) and that 53254e08, f6e16925, cee034f9 used before and independently of any refusal. The refused launch was the deviation from it.
- The engineering reason stands without the check: a Builder holds no connector, PostgREST runs no DDL (`grep` of `scripts/` finds no SQL-executing RPC), so "apply over REST" was never executable; and the down capture, the grant re-read and the before-image are owned by the cycle, which is the orchestrator.
- The rule's other half removes a path rather than adding one: with no connector the step stops and the ticket parks. Cycle 2ed22ad6 met exactly that case (connector answering Service Unavailable) and moved its ledger to PostgREST; under this rule a migration never follows it there.
- It does NOT license sending AGT-570's migration. That ticket carries one refusal and an open attempt 2 whose risky stage was held out; whether its stage may be sent is the redesign ledger's question (F3), not this ticket's.

If a future cycle finds the orchestrator's own connector apply refused, the honest answer becomes the other half of the ticket: such tickets park at design. The fact line is where that would be written, and the verifier clause is where it would be enforced.

## 5. Alternatives weighed

1. **A lint over the Builder launch text.** Closest to where the refusal fires, but the launch text is free prose the orchestrator types into the Agent tool; no script assembles it, so there is nothing to lint without a new assembly step in `runner-cycle.md` (380,973 of 381,000 bytes). Rejected: more files, and a lint whose job is to shape a launch is too near "wording the step".
2. **A line in `redesign-after-refusal.md`.** That file is read only after a refusal. The ticket asks for the park to happen at design, before one.
3. **Store every Builder launch text.** The real instrument gap (F1), but a new write path; it is its own ticket and proves nothing by itself.
4. **Chosen: the environment-facts register plus one verifier clause.** The register is rendered into every Designer prompt (`ds-knowledge-environment`, `--write-row`), so the rule reaches design. `--check-kickoff` is the gate every kickoff already passes at runbook step 6, so a kickoff that names an apply without saying who applies is refused before any Builder exists. 3 files, 4 tasks, no migration.

What it does not do: it does not apply AGT-570; it does not explain refusal #3; it does not store launch text; it does not touch the confirmation timeout.

Known cost: a kickoff stamped v7.0.1057 or later that mentions `apply_migration` only in passing must also carry the line (about 70 bytes). Parallel Designers whose prompt was assembled before T4's row write will meet the refusal once; its reason text carries the exact line.

Agent-row gate, read live: `node scripts/agent-row-gate.js --ticket=AGT-673 --action=edit-active` -> BUILD (`scope_origin = 'john-named'`), owing its own before-image, which `--write-row` writes.
