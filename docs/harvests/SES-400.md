## 2. Where the fix has to land, and why the obvious placement fails

Check 9 does not read a character window. `enclosingBlock()` (`scripts/check-session-docs.js`) expands from the hit to the nearest `BLOCK_START_RE` boundary, and that regex breaks on `\n[ \t]*\*\*` — a bold lead-in.

Line 163 *begins* with `   **`. So the block containing the `B35` hit **starts at line 163 itself** and ends before the paragraph at line 174 — 868 bytes, measured by replicating `enclosingBlock` against the stripped text. A retirement note written on line 162, which is where a reader would naturally put it (it is the same sentence), lands outside the window and leaves the FLAG exactly where it was.

This is the fact most likely to cost a later session a cycle, so it is the line filed to `environment-facts.md` in task 3.

The other three `B35` mentions in the file — lines 384, 1070, 1265 — already carry retirement vocabulary in their blocks and are not flagged. Line 1265 is the model the ticket points at: it names B35's answer (2) as *"explicitly unaffected and still binding"* after `M6-07`. The fix gives line 163 the same note in fewer bytes.

## 4. The QA, and why check 9 alone cannot be it

The ticket's warning is correct and it is now measured rather than argued. Reducing line 163 to `**\`America/Chicago\`** month (the month boundary is John's clock, never UTC).` — deleting the citation outright — yields **0 check-9 findings** at 380,886 bytes. Deletion is green, cheaper in bytes, and destroys the provenance of a ruling John made in person.

So a QA that asserts only "check 9 passes" grades a vacuum, and so would a regression test built the same way. Arm (c) exists to make the difference visible: it runs the deleted variant through the real check, asserts it **passes** (a), and asserts the citation assertion (b) **fails** on it. Arm (b) imports `RETIREMENT_VOCAB` instead of hardcoding `superseded`, so a later rewording that still marks the retirement keeps the test green while a deletion cannot.

Arm (a) calls the exported `checkRetiredRulesInLiveVoice` rather than shelling out to the gate, and never re-implements it — STANDARDS §4's rule against a test that is a second implementation agreeing with itself.

## 5. Scope, and what this does not do

Caps are P9 rung 2: 3 files, 4 tasks. Used in full, and the third file earns its place — the block-window fact is the one thing here that no reading of the ticket or the code makes obvious, which is `environment-facts.md`'s stated admission test.

Deliberately out of scope:

- **No dedup of the three duplicate procedure blocks** check 13 reports inside this runbook (lines 1851, 2844, 2982). It would free real bytes and is tempting under a 97-byte ceiling, but it edits executable procedure text in a governance runbook for a P9 citation fix. It is a separate ticket, and it is check 13 — not gating.
- **No move of the B35 passage to its canonical home** (`docs/RUNNER-GOV-0820-REQUIREMENTS.md#B35`, per the registry row). Check 12 territory, and it would be a rewrite, not a repair.
