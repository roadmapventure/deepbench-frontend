## Why seven anchors and not three

`AGT-226` shipped in this session (`v7.0.670`, `837a8a5a`): a kickoff may declare the exact
literals its build will edit, and `--check-kickoff` resolves each with `git grep -F -l`. The
ticket names three *files*, but `CLAUDE-DESIGN.md` L90-94 carries five independent strings
that must each change, and a kickoff that declared only "CLAUDE-DESIGN.md L90-94" would hand
the Builder a line range that drifts. So the anchors are declared per *string*: one for the
Auditor runbook, one for the gate review, five for the design defaults. Each was measured at
`855151f7` on a tree that does not contain this kickoff, which is what the checker's
`:(exclude)docs/kickoffs` / `:(exclude)docs/harvests` pathspecs require.

Verified before handback: `node scripts/verifier.js --check-kickoff=<draft> --json` →
exit 0, 8,187 bytes of 8,192, `anchors_declared: 7`, `anchors_graded: 7`,
`builder_model: claude-opus-5`.

## The deliberate exceptions, and why they are the same shape as AGT-147's

`AGT-147` kept `--model claude-fable-5-1` in the meter probe because only a Fable call returns
`seven_day_overage_included` (`SES-398`) — a literal that is load-bearing, not a default. The
equivalent on this side is different in kind but the same in rule: a model name that is
**history or rationale** is not "the model to use", and removing it would be rewriting the
record rather than removing a hardcode (pattern:90, pattern:166). Five such places stay
verbatim:

- `CLAUDE-DESIGN.md` L90 — John's quoted 2026-08-01 sentence. His words; the ticket says so.
- `CLAUDE-DESIGN.md` L91 — "this is the only tier where Fable quota is well spent", the
  rationale for the tier, not the tier's value.
- `CLAUDE-DESIGN.md` L92 — the *Corrected 2026-08-01* narrative, which explains why an
  omitted `model` param burned Fable quota. Deleting the name destroys the lesson.
- `CLAUDE-DESIGN.md` L278 — the cautionary omitted-`model`-param example. Out of scope by the
  ticket's own text, which names L90-94.
- `docs/runbooks/gate-review.md` line 1 — the `SES-313` stamp, a record of a past edit.

## Why no new test, and what that leaves open

None of the seven literals is asserted anywhere. Read for them and found zero hits:
`agt-70-auditor`, `agt-86h-auditor-routine`, `ses-286b-decision-runbooks`,
`ses-312-succession-without-cards`, `ses-285-m6-autonomy`, `ses-313-model-lanes`,
`SES-76-kickoff-dryrun-rule`, `scripts/check-model-ids.js`, `scripts/check-session-docs.js`.
`ses-313`'s doc assertion targets `docs/RUNNER-GOV-0820-REQUIREMENTS.md`, not `gate-review.md`.

`AGT-147`'s own split filed these three as untested, independent, human-read lines for exactly
that reason. Standing up a regression test for three prose lines is the parallel structure
pattern:17 warns against when a structure that fits already exists — and one does: the anchors
block. After the build removes the strings, re-running `--check-kickoff` on the committed
kickoff must refuse, because the only remaining copies are inside the excluded directories.
That is the discriminating QA, and it costs nothing to run.

What it does **not** do: it grades this ticket's change once, at this ship. It does not stop a
future session from re-typing a model id into these files. A standing guard for that —
something in the shape of `check-model-ids.js` extended to governance docs — is a real gap and
a separate ticket; it is not filed here because it is a general mechanism and pattern:65 says
park it rather than widen this one.

## The named deviation: orchestrator, not mechanical

The ticket text says "mechanical lane (claude-sonnet-5)", inherited from `AGT-147`'s own
kickoff. That is wrong for an unattended build now: `runner-cycle.md` step 7 runs the Builder
on the `orchestrator` lane, and `scripts/verifier.js`'s `kickoffBuilderModelFinding`
(`AGT-189`) refuses a kickoff whose `Model:` line names anything but the live `orchestrator`
row. The work really is mechanical; the lane is not the Designer's to re-route. Declared
`claude-opus-5`, named as a deviation in the kickoff's SESSION section and again in its STOP
LINE, reversible.
