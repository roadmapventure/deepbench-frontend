# SES-383 harvest — why one shape, and what the other two leave broken

## The measurement that decided the design

The ticket names three candidate fixes. Only one combination actually satisfies its own closing
requirement, and the reason is a live property of the frontend that the ticket does not mention.

`src/hooks/useAIActivity.js` prices rows at READ time whenever `cost_usd` is NULL. Two sites, both
read live this turn:

- line 262, `buildActivitySummary` (raw Supabase rows, the CHI/agent rollup)
- line 1164, `hydrateFromSupabase` (the AI Audit's own entries)

both of the form `paired.has(row.id) ? 0 : (row.cost_usd != null ? parseFloat(row.cost_usd) :
computeCallCost(row.model, row.input_tokens, row.output_tokens, row.cache_creation_input_tokens,
row.cache_read_input_tokens))`. `byService` (1491) and `totalCost` (1562) fold those values.

The LOG-149 comment at line 1043 states the intent plainly: "Deriving cost from tokens at read time
prices every existing and future row with no backfill migration." That was right for LOG-149 and is
exactly wrong here. It means **ticket option (a) on its own — agent-log.js writes NULL — moves the
audit's dollar view by $0.00**, today and after any backfill: the browser simply re-derives
$220.3960 from the 43,688,839 tokens. A designer who stopped at the ticket's option list would have
shipped a change that passes a SQL assertion and leaves the screen identical.

## Why each single option fails, and what the chosen combination is

- **(a) alone** — audit unchanged (above). Even setting the read-time issue aside, the wall would
  still be a separate aggregation over `runner_cycles`, so one billable `mcp`/`script` row re-opens
  the gap. Two numbers again.
- **(b) alone** — wall reads the log's billable sources, but the audit keeps pricing session rows.
  $220.40 on screen against $0.00 at the wall: the ticket's closing requirement fails by definition.
- **(c) alone** — cycles write `api_cost_*` from the log at close-out. This *adds* a derived copy of
  a number that already exists, keeps two stores in sync by procedure, and inherits whatever the
  log's session rows say. It is the most work and the most drift surface. Declined.

Chosen: **(a) + (b) + a backfill + the read-time guard**, which is cheaper than it sounds because
(a) is one property and the guard is one clause extended at two existing call sites. The invariant
afterwards is a single sentence: *`ai_activity_log.cost_usd` is the dollar number; a NULL there means
not billable; both the audit and the wall sum that column and nothing else.* No `call_source` filter
is needed at either end, because NULL adds nothing to a SUM — that is what makes it one number
rather than two filters someone has to keep in step.

## Why NULL and not 0, restated for the builder who will be tempted

Writing `cost_usd = 0` on session rows would make the audit read $0 with **no frontend change at
all** (`row.cost_usd != null` is true for 0). It is the cheapest thing that makes the screen right
and it is wrong: `lib/activity-log.js:121-126` documents the three outcomes deliberately — an
explicit 0 is the caller asserting "billable and free" (the refused-call seam), `undefined` means
"price it from my tokens", NULL means unknown/not-billable. §19v's own LOG-149 amendment rests on
that distinction (a refused call costs $0; an aborted one is billed). Collapsing it to buy one
skipped file would trade a documented semantic for a screen fix, and the next session reading a
$0.00 session row could not tell it from a refusal. The guard at two lines is the honest price.

## The backfill, and why it is not optional

The orchestrator's constraint is the ticket's: the two numbers must AGREE. A forward-only fix leaves
today's 14 rows priced at $220.3960 and all-time 78 rows at $323.0360 sitting in the column both
readers now trust. The audit would show them (read-time guard notwithstanding, the SQL wall would
not) and any ad-hoc `sum(cost_usd)` — `scripts/audit-ledger.js`, a future briefing card, John at a
SQL prompt — would read phantom dollars. So: **yes, backfilling**, 78 rows, and per §19v every
mutated row gets a `runner_before_images` row carrying the full prior row via `to_jsonb(r)` FIRST,
inside the same transaction, after `capture_migration_down()`. `docs/SESSIONS.md:480` is the shape to
copy (SES-373's five-row rollback-backfill, decision `6c19edb2`, images then UPDATEs in one block).

## The bounded gap this design accepts

Pre-LOG-149 rows (written before v7.0.450, 2026-09-11) carry NULL `cost_usd` and are still priced
from tokens by the audit, while the wall's SQL counts them as $0. §19v explicitly keeps that
fallback alive for those rows, so removing it is a different ticket. Today the gap is exactly $0.00:
all 337 non-session rows in the Chicago day carry 0 tokens, and `computeCallCost` returns null for a
token-less row (LOG-149's "unknown is not free"). The window ages out of a day and a month wall on
its own. Stated rather than discovered later.
