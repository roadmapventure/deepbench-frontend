## §Why — the recorded turn, not a re-build

The ticket text says "re-runs each on the candidate model in a scratch branch with no push and no ledger writes". Two honest variants were weighed:

1. **Full re-build.** Re-run the whole cycle (design + build + verify) on the candidate in a scratch worktree. Cost per trial: 34–65 minutes and 60k–2M tokens (the Builder turn alone logged 131.6M tokens over 75 rows in 14 days, ~1.75M per turn). Three trials per job type is ~3 hours and 3–6 pace points of the 14.29 daily limit — the room gate would refuse it on most days, and a build whose push is refused cannot reach the stage the real job reached, so "same or later stage" would be unscorable by construction. It also cannot be made write-free: the Builder's contract pushes, `verifier.js` records a verdict row, and every driver logs.
2. **Recorded-turn replay (chosen).** Re-assemble the job's one agent turn through the same `agent-prompt.js` path, at the tree as it stood when the cycle started (`git rev-list -1 --before=<started_at> origin/dev`, detached scratch worktree), and run it once on the candidate with `claude -p --model <candidate>` under a read-only tool allowlist. Cost per trial is one turn (the recorded Fable design turns are 95k in / 14–22k out). Writes are impossible rather than forbidden: `--allowedTools "Read,Grep,Glob,Bash(node scripts/baseline-red-set.js:*)"` refuses `git push`, `agent-log.js`, every MCP and every write tool (pattern 10, 19). The only row a trial writes is its own `ai_activity_log` usage row, which §19k requires and the ticket asks for ("trials logged as usage"), under feature `model-assignment:trial:depth0` so it can never be picked as a future baseline job.

What the chosen variant does not prove: ship quality. A replayed design turn shows the candidate can produce a contract-valid, cap-compliant kickoff (or a dead-premise finding with evidence) at a measured token cost; it does not show the kickoff would have built. That is stated in the evidence (`tools: "read-only, no database"`, `verdict: 'none'` on both sides) rather than hidden. The `verdict` limb of AGT-144's switch rule (`baseline.verdict <> 'approve' or candidate.verdict = 'approve'`) is then satisfied identically on both sides, and the decision rests on `passed` and the 10% token limb — which is the honest reading of a turn-level trial. `hard` and `clearly_better` are absent from the runner's output on purpose: they are the manager's judgment, set on the evidence before `apply_model_assignment()` is called (AGT-144 harvest §B.4).

Why the candidate's baseline `passed` is `cycle.outcome !== 'failed'`: the real turn's kickoff was accepted and the cycle proceeded on it; a `failed` cycle is the one outcome that says the turn did not carry. `gated_before_build` counts as passed — the gate is a ruling on the ticket, not on the kickoff.

Why the replay runs without the database: the recorded Designer had a read-only Supabase MCP; `claude -p` in a fresh scratch worktree has none, and `.env.local` is deliberately not copied (no credential inside a trial). The candidate is therefore held to a slightly harder standard than the baseline. Stated in the evidence; not corrected, because correcting it means handing a trial a service key.

## §Replayable — one entry today, and why it is a table

`REPLAYABLE` maps an `ai_activity_log.feature` to the `--agent/--capability/--intent` triple and, implicitly, the task_context shape the runbook prints for that step. Today it holds `design-kickoff:ds-kickoff-intent:depth0` only — the judgment lane's most frequent turn (57 Fable rows in 14 days) and the exact turn AGT-146's first Opus 5.5 trial is for. The turns that were considered and left out, each for a reason a later ticket can remove:

- `build-ticket:bd-build-intent:depth0` (orchestrator lane): the turn's contract is a pushed tree; read-only replay cannot reach it. A trial of the orchestrator lane needs its own design (a scratch-branch build with the push refused and the suite as the score) — a separate ticket, not a `REPLAYABLE` entry.
- `verify-ship:vf-verdict-intent:depth0`: needs the verifier's context JSON for a past ship (`--judge=session` pass one runs the gates on a tree); reconstructable, one entry plus a base-tree checkout at `push_sha`. Deferred.
- `run-project:dm-run-intent:depth0`, `decide-gated-card` (`dm-gate-intent`): each driver already has a `--prepare` door that prints the prompt with no writes; the task_context is the live board, not a past one, so a "replay" would not be the recorded job. Deferred until a driver stores its prepare JSON.
- `bench-report-card`, `data-room-custody` (the two capability rows): task_context is a trace / a library query the log does not carry. `--job-kind=capability` for either exits 4 `not-replayable` today, by name, rather than trialling something else and calling it the job.

The structural fix that makes every turn replayable — `agent-log.js` storing the turn's `task_context` in `call_facts` — is one flag plus a runbook edit at the byte ceiling (380,902 of 381,000). Not this ticket; named here for whoever files it (pattern 96, 120).

Task_context reconstruction for a design turn: the `backlog_items` row is read as it is now, not as it was; a ticket's `description` can be edited after its cycle. The evidence's `job_ref` carries both ids so a reader can compare. `caps` is `{files:3,tasks:4}` — the class caps the cycle row's notes carried are not stored in a parseable field; every design turn in the 29 baseline rows ran under 3/4 (their kickoffs say so).

## Room gate

`roomFor(detail, candidate)` reads `runner_should_boot().detail` exactly as the AGT-144 Knowledge row states it, plus one check the row implies: a reading older than `meter_stale_hours` is no reading (pattern 165 — a budget rule is only as good as its sensor). The gate is read once before the first replay; three turns at ~120k tokens each cannot cross a pace point, so re-reading between them would be a knob without a bound (pattern 67).

## §Amend — AGT-144's Knowledge row, step 2

AGT-144 harvest §B step 2 says "Each job is re-run on the candidate in a scratch branch … the outcome is `passed` when the re-run reaches the same or a later stage than the real job did; the Verifier verdict is taken from a Verifier pass over the re-run." The chosen variant satisfies the scratch branch, no push, no ledger writes, tokens and minutes; it does not run a Verifier pass and its `passed` is the turn's contract, not a stage. Because AGT-144 builds before AGT-148, the cheapest correct fix is to amend §B step 2 before its migration is applied: *"Each job is the lane's recorded agent turn for that cycle, re-assembled and run once on the candidate in a scratch worktree with read-only tools: no push, no `runner_cycles`, no `runner_decisions`, no `backlog_items` write, no ship. Tokens and minutes are measured; `passed` means the answer met the turn's own contract (every required key; a kickoff within the byte cap); `verdict` is `none` on both sides for a turn trial."* One sentence, same row, no new file. If AGT-144 has already applied its migration when this is read, the amendment is a `skill_profiles.method` edit under an agent-row decision in the AGT-148 build — a row write, not a file, so it stays within the cap; the attended session decides which (pattern 94: amend the locked text in the same sitting, never silently patch against it).
