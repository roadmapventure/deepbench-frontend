## 2. Why it is silent, precisely

`fingerprint()` (`scripts/audit-ledger.js:195`) is `sha256(kind | sorted location keys | normalize(governing_fact))`.
`censusFindingFor` (`scripts/ticket-owner.js:1020`) deliberately keeps the count out of `governing_fact`
and puts it in the location `text` — its own comment explains why (a count in the fact would mint a new
finding every night the number moved). Correct against nightly churn, and it is also why a `ticketed`
slug can grow from 89 to 103 to 1,000 without ever reaching the weekly list again. That is the fence the
ticket asks for, and it is one string away.

`audit-ledger.js`'s `ruled-out` test matches a `not-a-defect` row by fingerprint **or** by sharing 2+
location *homes*. An `owner:` finding carries exactly one location, so 2+ is unreachable: bands cannot be
suppressed by a prior ruling, and a band crossing always mints a genuinely new finding.

## 3. The per-check ruling, and where I disagree with the ticket

The ticket proposes retiring `actual-unknown` (then 89, now 103) **and** `size-missing` (then 31, now 33)
as "estimate hygiene, not defects". I checked both rather than accepting either.

**`actual-unknown` — retire.** Two independent reasons.
(a) Unanswerable by construction: the finding fires only when a closed row has `cost_pct_snapshot` null
*and* `ticket_matrix.actual_cycles` = 0, which means no cycle row was ever written (an attended or desktop
session). Its own detail at `ticket-owner.js:418` says so. No capability, model or human can recover a
cost that was never recorded.
(b) `cost_pct_snapshot` has **no reader**: grep over the whole tree less `node_modules`/`dist` returns
`scripts/ticket-owner.js`, three regression tests, two fixtures, the AGT-79 seed SQL and four docs. No
screen, API route, brief or script reads it. A flag nobody can answer, about a column nobody reads.

**`size-missing` — keep, against the ticket.** `size_stamp` is not inert: `scripts/build-briefing.mjs:638`
selects it and `:676` counts remaining Selfbuild work by it. 33 nulls silently shrink a breakdown John
reads (pattern:41 — never exclude real work from a user-visible count). `scripts/tripwire-to-backlog.js:262,401`
writes it at filing time, so the gap is a filing defect with a live writer, not hygiene. Recorded here
rather than obeyed silently (pattern:94).

**The other eleven stay.** Each names a real column a live writer owns: `quote-missing` (the quote the
Prioritizer sets), `verdict-missing` (`runner_verdicts` at ship), `designed-closed`, `type-off-taxonomy`,
`delivered-unaccepted` (an Accept decision), `cycles-over-quote`, `remainder-stranded` (whose detail
already carries `settle-ship.js --resettle`, AGT-167), `claim-on-closed`, `claim-expired`,
`cost-snapshot-missing`, `unrevalidated-30d`.

Net: 103 of 259 rows (40%) drain on the ruling; 156 survive under the fence.

## 4. Why the ruling unit is the check and never the row

`openJudgmentBySlug`'s own comment is the argument, and it was written before the pile doubled: "222 open
judgment rows over 10 checks are 10 questions for a capability to answer, and 222 ledger rows would be a
queue nobody reads." Nothing about 259 changes that. A per-row ruler would also need a new verdict schema
on the judge's intent row — which this ticket may not touch (§6).

## 5. Alternatives rejected

- **Extend the nightly judge (`--judge`) to rule open ledger rows.** The natural reading of "wire the
  ruler", and blocked: `ingestJudgment` validates against the Intent's own stored schema
  (`validateAgentVerdict(state.schema, answer)`), which lives on the agent/intent row.
  `node scripts/agent-row-gate.js --ticket=AGT-169 --action=edit-active` returns
  `GATED — no agreed-ticket authority — scope_origin is "discovered"`. Recorded, not routed around.
- **Delete the retired rows.** The ticket says "delete its rows"; `planWrites`'s own contract says
  "Clearing is not deletion — a cleared row keeps its history and simply stops being open", and pattern:54
  says resolution collapses an artifact and never destroys it. Clearing also needs no new code at all:
  `clear = prior − tonight`, so a check that stops filing drains itself on the next `--apply` night, under
  that night's existing reversible decision. Deviation from the ticket's literal word, stated.
- **A `RETIRED` list in code only.** Retires today's check and leaves every future retirement needing a
  code change — bounding the problem instead of eliminating it (pattern:1, pattern:2). The union in
  `retiredChecks()` keeps one core (pattern:14) while giving the weekly review real power.
- **A `not-a-defect` ruling written by this build into `audit_findings`.** `public.apply_audit_review()`
  owns that write, its before-images and its decision (§19b); hand-PATCHing around it to save one
  constant would be exactly the routing-around this platform refuses. The constant records the Designer's
  call; the RPC stays the only writer of a *reviewer's* call.
- **A remedy sentence in every surviving check's detail** (AGT-167's shape, generalised). Real value, but
  it adds no ruling power and costs seven per-check strings; left for whoever next touches those checks.

## 6. The fence, sized

`FENCE_BANDS = [25, 50, 100, 200, 400]`, a fixed declared constant rather than a measurement
(pattern:18). Against today's board the first run after the ship raises exactly two new weekly findings —
`verdict-missing` (45 → past 25) and `size-missing` (33 → past 25) — while `delivered-unaccepted` (24)
stays byte-identical and does not. A band is coarse on purpose: a check oscillating 24↔25 falls back to
the unbanded fingerprint, which is an already-`ticketed` row, so it re-files as `seen`, never as new.

## 8. How the ticket closes

`done` this cycle. Its three sentences are: decide per check (§3, on measurements, with the one
disagreement recorded), wire the ruler (a `not-a-defect` weekly ruling now retires a check and drains its
rows), and fence what survives (§6). The 156 surviving rows are not residue to refile — they are the kept
checks' ordinary population, and the fence is what now watches them (pattern:95).
