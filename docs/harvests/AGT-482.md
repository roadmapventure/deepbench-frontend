## 2. The design, and what was rejected

**One home.** `public.claim_exclusion_reason(text, timestamptz)` holds three facts that are one fact:
the hours, the sentinel and the sentence. It returns the exclusion sentence or NULL, so a caller asks
one question - "does this claim keep the ticket out?" - instead of re-deriving the predicate
(pattern:14, pattern:15). It reuses `countable_claim_hours()` rather than introducing a second 6
(pattern:2, pattern:18); AGT-481's own kickoff said in its STOP LINE that AGT-482 would reuse it.

NULL-handling is taken from the live bodies, not invented: in `pick_exclusions()` a row whose
`claimed_by` is set and whose `claimed_at` is NULL reads as live (the `coalesce(..., false)` makes
the comparison fail), and in `countable_tickets_per_project()` the same row is excluded. F1 keeps
both by testing `p_claimed_at IS NULL OR p_claimed_at >= now() - ...`.

**Rejected: a `claim_is_stopped(text)` sibling.** Two functions would have let `backlog_mode()` ask
only the age question, but it splits the sentinel across two homes for the sake of one caller that
this ticket does not touch. One function, one home.

**Rejected: changing `backlog_mode()`.** It paints the "in development" badge. Pointing it at F1
makes AGT-020, AGT-159 and PRO-3 read "in development" forever, which is both wrong and a visible
change John has not seen (pattern:84, pattern:90). The ticket's own words scope the change to "the
pick and the countable function". So `backlog_mode()` and `runner_settings.claim_stale_hours` (24)
are deliberately untouched, and OD-08 is rewritten to say that 24 now serves the badge alone.
Consequence worth stating: after this ship the platform holds two claim numbers with two homes and
two meanings. That is the honest state, and the guard asserts both in one run so nobody later
"tidies" them into one.

**`drain_epic_next`'s peer counter.** Its sentence `'; %s held by a live peer claim'` stays
byte-unchanged while a STOPPED member now counts toward it. A stopped claim IS a hold, so the count
is right; the word "peer" is imprecise and rewording a displayed sentence is out of this ticket's
scope. Named in the STOP LINE as a reversible Designer call.

**`STOP_CLAIM_PREFIX` is a declared second copy.** PostgREST cannot call a SQL function inside a
filter, so the driver's atomic claim cannot ask F1. The alternative - routing the claim through a new
RPC - is a bigger build than this ticket, and rule B40's atomic single-statement claim is the thing
it would have to re-implement. So the prefix is exported as a named constant, commented as a copy,
and T2 arm E pins it equal to the SQL home by round-tripping it through
`rpc/claim_exclusion_reason`. A checked copy, not a silent one.
