## Decisions and alternatives

1. **Ladder position 2, right after `scheduler_off`.** A refusal must name the actionable cause. When the sensor is stale, the wall and the pace are computed from a number the gate has just declared untrustworthy; naming `weekly_pace` on a stale 25% is the exact misreport that let 22 ships run. Alternative considered: after `weekly_pace` (so an over-pace stale reading still says "pace") — rejected, because the fix for a stale meter is the reader, and the reason should point there. Precedence 1 over 2 is asserted by fixture (e).
2. **NULL-safe, not NULL-as-stale.** With no reading at all `reading_age_hours` is NULL and the new `WHEN` falls through, exactly as the wall and the pace do. Treating an absent reading as stale would change the fail-open-on-empty-table behaviour SES-297 chose deliberately; the table has 34 rows and is never empty in practice. Out of scope, stated.
3. **Threshold lives in `runner_settings`, John's number, default 2.** The reader writes every 30 min (SES-374), so 2h is four missed readings. A `CHECK (> 0)` stops a zero from refusing every boot. The ticket's own QA (set to 6, the 3h reading passes) is why it is a column and not a literal.
4. **M5-15 must be rewritten, not left standing.** Its statement says staleness "never refuses a run" and "no other gate carries its own staleness threshold" — both become false at this ship. The register is authoritative and byte-identical across `governance_rules`, the doc and the snapshot (check-session-docs checks 9–11). The history in that section (SES-280 refused at 24h and live-blocked the drain because only John could refresh the reading) is what changed: the reading is now machine-written, so a refusal on staleness is a refusal on a dead sensor, not on John's typing. SES-302's real lesson — one home per consequence — is preserved: the refusal has one home (the gate), the ceiling has one home (RUNG 2).
5. **No new header stamp on the runbook.** `grep -c '^<!-- DeepBench v'` = 5, the check-7 cap `ses-297` asserts. Adding one forces retiring the oldest verbatim into `docs/SESSIONS.md` — a fourth hand-edited file. The inline `<!-- FEATURE: SES-389 -->` marker is the precedent at runner-cycle.md L3872.
6. **The brief/STATE rendering half is residue, declared, not built.** `render-standing-brief.js` reads `report_card_usage` only; `render-claude-state.js` reads no usage row; `build-briefing.mjs:357` reads `all_models_pct` alone. Nothing renders the percentage in the two files the ticket names, so "beside the percentage" has no anchor, and adding one is two more scripts over a 3-file cap. The gate's `detail` (`reading_taken_at`, `reading_age_hours`, `meter_stale_hours`) is written verbatim into every refusal's `runner_cycles.notes`, which is the surface a reader actually opens. Not filed as a ticket: it is the ticket's own text, for John's ship decision, not a discovery.
7. **Files counted: 3 hand-edited repo files** — runbook, test, register doc. The migration lives in Supabase (no `supabase/` directory in the repo; `schema_migrations` holds `ses297`, `ses302`, `ses368` the same way). `cycle-card.md`, `RULES-SNAPSHOT.md` and `BACKLOG-SNAPSHOT.md` are generated views (`verifier.js` DIFF_LAST, `PROCEDURE_GENERATED_DOCS`), re-rendered the way SES-368's ship did under the same cap.
