The 89 open rows span three CST days (2026-09-15 → 2026-09-18), interleaved by `expires_at`, so nothing on the page tells John what was decided *yesterday* or *today*. 25 decisions were made in the last 24h (`ticket-status` 8, `rollback` 6, `agent-row` 5, `ticket-scope` 2, and one each of `hygiene`, `learning`, `rule`, `classification`); 204 in 7 days. Verdict: **premise alive**. The gap is real, narrow, and closable in one cycle.

## Why this shape, and what it deliberately does not do

- **A pure group, not a new table.** Seven groups in this file already follow one contract: `renderX(facts, stamp[, nowIso])` is pure, the guard drives it from fixtures, `fetchFacts()` reads, `factsSha` canonicalises, and "absent" is never rendered as zero. Slice 3 adds an eighth on the same terms. No DDL means no migration-down capture, no grant surface, and no `runner_settings` column.
- **The day window is a DISPLAY constant, not a cadence.** SES-146's "every cadence number is a column" governs numbers `record_decision()` computes from; SES-334 explicitly exempted `OPEN_DECISION_BATCH` as display. `DAILY_DECISION_LINES = 25` and the 7-day table are the same kind of number.
- **A hard line cap, not a per-kind collapse.** SES-334's batch collapse bounds one kind per day; it does not bound the total. The brief went 18 KB → 149 KB when the Prioritizer wrote 567 decisions in a day, and this group would have printed them again. A flat cap of 25 bullets plus a `select` for the rest bounds the group absolutely.
- **CST, because John reads CST.** `cstDay()` uses `en-CA` for a sortable `YYYY-MM-DD` key; `cst()` already exists for display. A UTC day boundary would file the 23:30 CST decisions of a busy evening under the next morning — the single most likely silent defect here, which is why it is test arm 1.
- **No second undo surface.** The daily group prints no `reverse_decision(` line. Two places offering to reverse the same row is the duplication `Open decisions` already owns.
- **Not the briefing page.** John's daily reading also happens in `src/`, but a UI change needs his word; the ticket says "on the standing brief" and that is where it lands.

## Alternatives considered and dropped

1. *Re-key `Open decisions` itself by day.* Rejected: it would delete the reversal-window view John's `M6-02` undo flow depends on, and `ses-286c` guards it.
2. *A `daily_decisions` view in Postgres.* Rejected: DDL, grants and a migration-down for a grouping three lines of JS already do from a read the file makes anyway.
3. *Yesterday-only.* Rejected: the brief re-renders at every ship, so a fixed "yesterday" is empty each morning and stale each evening. The newest day that has rows, plus a 7-day table, reads correctly at any hour.

## Slice 4 (not designed here)

A `questions_to_john_week` scoreboard column, with `outcome_claim_is_valid` gaining it — the weekly count of questions that still reached John, target zero. Slice 3 renders that count on the brief but does not score it; slice 4 turns it into an outcome the platform is graded on. Slice 2 (routing the 19 moved-to-manager asks onto SES-402's decision rows) stays gated while SES-402 is open and waits on SES-378's John-owned part.
