## 2. Alternatives, and why the kickoff takes the one it does

- **Runbook prose alone.** Rejected. This platform's own record (`SES-116`, `SES-129`, `SES-86` phase 3) is that a rule each cycle must remember is a rule that gets silently skipped — and the 89-of-118 historical rate is that lesson again. Pattern 10: when the correct value exists deterministically, no writer is put in charge of it.
- **A `scripts/verifier.js` key that checks reachability with git.** Rejected, and it is the tempting one. The verifier runs at step 7a, *before* the push and rebase, so at that moment a note's artifact shas are pre-rebase **by construction** and every cycle would fail. And CI clones at depth 1 (`SES-393`), so a git-history check there goes NOT RUN — a green that grades nothing.
- **A second trigger, or a new table of recorded artifacts.** Rejected. `runner_record_citation_guard()` already fires `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE` on exactly the two tables and the eight columns that carry this text; a second block inside it is the whole change (pattern 17, and `v7.0.644`'s own shape).
- **Refusing every hex token.** Rejected: notes legitimately carry base shas, peers' pushes and CI shas. The match is anchored to a *commit claim* and allows any push/graded sha, which is why arm B re-inserts three real live texts and expects acceptance.
- **Adding a runbook header stamp.** Rejected on cost: it spends ~200 B of the 1,007 available, forces a rotation (and with it an `SES-164` step-2 relocation of the dropped stamp's zero-hit facts), and drags `ses-424c`'s `LATER_STAMP` and `agt-133`'s line-1 assertion into the diff. `v7.0.645` edited the runbook this morning with no stamp; this ship follows it.

## 4. What this ship does not do

- It cannot catch a **wrong path** — only a sha that no push ever produced. A path is at least checkable against the tree by a reader; a rewritten sha is not.
- The **grandfather clause** leaves all 89 historical tokens in place, deliberately: the alternative is refusing every future append to 71 cycles' notes.
- R2 (the exclusion reason from `prime_directive_queue()`'s own predicate), R3 (completeness graded in `verifier.js`, non-gating — 41 omissions over ~30 cards would brick the ship step) and R4 (notes completeness) stand exactly as `v7.0.644` filed them.
- Ticket hygiene, checked rather than assumed: `AGT-173`'s `design_status` is NULL and `kickoff_link` still names the spent `v7.0.644` kickoff. That is the shipped convention for a settled ship, not a loose end; step 6 of this cycle sets both to this kickoff in one guarded write.
