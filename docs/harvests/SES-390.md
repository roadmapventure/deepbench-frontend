## 2. The two variants the ticket offered, and why `GREATEST()` won

The ticket allows either (A) `max(all_models_pct, fable_pct)` against the existing wall and pace, or (B) `fable_pct` against its own `runner_budget.fable_rest_pct` (default = `weekly_rest_pct`).

(A) is the cheapest variant that proves the claim: no schema change, no new John number, no table-object in the down capture, and the pace applies to Fable for free (the ticket asks for both walls). (B) adds a column John has not asked to move — its default equals the rest wall, so on day one it is (A) with an extra row to keep consistent, and it gives the pace no Fable counterpart at all (a second pace setting would be a third number). If John later wants the Fable rest at a different level than all-models, that is a one-column ticket on top of (A); nothing in (A) blocks it.

`GREATEST` is NULL-safe in the direction the ladder already relies on: `GREATEST(57, NULL) = 57`, `GREATEST(NULL, NULL) = NULL` → the wall and pace comparisons are NULL and fall through, exactly as a missing reading does today. A John-typed row without `fable_pct` therefore grades on all-models alone (QA case e), which is the pre-`SES-390` behaviour, not a new failure mode.

`gated_meter` ties go to `'all_models'` deliberately: the all-models meter is the number John named for the pace (M5-16, his words), so an equal reading is reported under the name he already reads.

## 3. What stays on `all_models_pct`, and why

- **Headroom / `unaffordable`.** `runner_pct_per_cycle()` is `percentile_cont(0.5)` of `pct_used / cycles` over `usage_window_cycles`, whose `pct_used` is an all-models delta (`usage_windows`). A ticket's `pct_of_week` is therefore priced in all-models percentage points; comparing it to `100 − fable_pct` would compare two different units. Left as is, and the runbook reason 7 now says so.
- **`resolve_day_token_cap()`.** Reads `v_reading.all_models_pct` into `v_meter` and reports `rest_wall_hit` — the *ceiling*'s view of the wall, consumed by step 3 and the briefing page's §4 guardrail line. Changing it is a second function, a second down capture and a second oracle; the boot decision is the gate's, and the gate refuses before the resolver runs on a Fable-walled cycle. Declared, not built. If John wants the resolver's `rest_wall_hit` to agree with the gate, that is a follow-on — but note the briefing page will show `rest_wall_hit = false` on a day the gate refused `weekly_wall`/`fable`; the §4 model table (task 5) is where he sees why.
- **`scripts/check-token-wall-binding.js` / `SES-161`.** Prices predicted tokens against all-models headroom for the same unit reason. Untouched.
- **Runbook step 3 lines 1292/1307/1393** describe the *ceiling*'s rest check (`all_models_pct ≥ weekly_rest_pct`), which is the resolver's, so they stay accurate.
