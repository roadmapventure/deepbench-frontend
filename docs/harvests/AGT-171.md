## Why fold the release into the close (and not the alternatives)

1. **Fuse (6) and (7) into one statement** — chosen. Postgres data-modifying CTE: the `runner_lease` release is the CTE, the `runner_cycles` close is the outer UPDATE, `RETURNING c.ended_at, (SELECT released_at FROM released) AS lease_released_at`. 1 row = closed; `lease_released_at NULL` = your lease was TTL-stolen (leave the new holder alone, as today). A cycle cannot close without attempting the release, and the leased span ends at (6) — before every sub-agent. `EXPLAIN` on live planned it: `Update on runner_cycles c` with `CTE released → Update on runner_lease` and `InitPlan 2 → CTE Scan on released`. Column types checked: `runner_lease.holder uuid`, `runner_cycles.id uuid`, `outcome` CHECK in (shipped, gated_before_build, reverted, did_not_run, failed). Block body 384 B ≤ `FULL_BLOCK_MAX` 400 (`scripts/render-cycle-card.js:38`), so the card can carry it in full.
2. **Grow the TTL** — rejected (the ticket says so too): a steal means a silent holder; a 30-minute TTL wedges the tail for 30 minutes on every silent cycle.
3. **Move (7e)/(7f) above the lease-take** — rejected: they review a FINISHED cycle (`reviewGate` exits 3 on `outcome NULL`, `audit-run-review.js:112-118`), so they must follow the close; the close must therefore release.
4. **Release in the `runner_cycles` close via a trigger** — rejected: a trigger is a migration plus a `runner_before_images` question for lease writes the runbook says need none ("the lease is ledger state, not content", `:337`); the one-statement form gets the same guarantee with zero schema change (pattern:8).
